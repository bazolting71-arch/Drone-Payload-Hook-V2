WHAT CHANGED IN v2.5 (hook was loose)
- Why it wobbled: the servo end of the hook is held only by the metal arm sitting in the hook's pocket,
  and that pocket had ~0.85 mm of play around the arm. The hook could also slide ~2+ mm toward the servo
  (more if your arm sits further out), and only 5 mm of the rod sits in the far bearing.
- Hook (part 3): 5 thin CRUSH RIBS around the arm's round hub and 4 on the walls of the arm slot. They
  squash as you push the arm in and grip it tight (fits hubs about 15-16.4 mm, arms about 9-10.6 mm wide).
  The bearing end is now 8.3 mm (was 8.0) in the frame's 8.6 mm hole, and has an M4 thread in its tip.
- RETAINER: one printed M4x14 bolt goes from OUTSIDE the far end wall, through the little sleeve (part 10)
  and into the thread in the hook's tip. The sleeve is 0.2 mm longer than the wall is thick, so the bolt
  locks to the hook but never clamps the wall: the hook still turns freely, but can't slide more than
  about 0.3 mm in or out, no matter where the servo arm sits. Checked in Fusion: 0.3 mm end play, 0.2 mm
  side play at the bearing, 0-90 deg swing touches nothing, stops still catch the disc first.

ASSEMBLY (changes)
1. Frame: if you already printed the v2.4 frame you don't need to reprint it - drill a 6.5 mm hole
   straight through the far end wall, behind the hook's bearing hole. On the OUTSIDE of that wall the
   centre is: middle of the wall side-to-side, 12 mm up from the wall's bottom edge. Drill 3 mm first,
   check it comes out in the middle of the bearing hole, then open it to 6.5 mm.
2. Push the metal arm into the hook's pocket firmly (or press the servo in) - the ribs should squash.
   If it really won't go, shave the ribs a little with a knife, don't sand the pocket walls.
3. Put the hook in as before (bearing end into the far hole), then from OUTSIDE the far wall: sleeve
   into the hole, M4x14 bolt through it into the hook. Hold the hook and tighten the bolt firmly.
   The hook must still turn by hand; if it drags, the sleeve is printed short - print the spare.
4. If the 8.3 mm bearing end is tight in the frame, ream the frame hole with an 8.5 mm bit by hand.

WHAT CHANGED IN v2.4 (why the rod snapped, and the fix)
- At CLOSED (0 deg) the hook's tip sits only 1.8 mm from the keeper rib on the frame. If the servo goes
  ~2 deg past 0 (or is sent to the wrong end), the hook jams against the frame and the full servo torque
  (up to 35 kg.cm) twists the 8 mm rod: about 34 MPa, more than printed PETG layers can take.
- Hook (part 3): the rod between the drive disc and the collar is now 14 mm thick (was 8) with 45 deg
  tapers at both ends instead of sharp steps, the collar is 18 mm (was 16), and the inside corner where
  the hook arm meets the collar has a brace. Same jam now = about 6 MPa in the rod (5x lower).
  The end that sits in the frame's bearing is still 8 mm, so the rest of the frame fit is unchanged.
- Frame (part 2): two small stop blocks on the inside of the servo wall. They catch the DRIVE DISC
  (the part the servo arm sits in) at about -1.25 deg and +96 deg, so if the servo over-travels it
  pushes against the frame directly and the rod and hook arm carry nothing.
  Checked in Fusion: on the closed side the disc hits its stop at -1.25 deg, the hook would only have
  reached the keeper rib at -2.25 deg; on the open side the stop catches at 96 deg (hook would hit the
  frame at about 140 deg). Normal 0-90 deg swing touches nothing.
- The new hook also fits the v2.2/v2.3 frame (no stops then). Print both for the full fix.

FIRMWARE: use firmware/Drone_Hook_HM10. The hook needs roughly a 70-90 deg swing from CLOSED to OPEN.
The exact servo numbers depend on how the arm sits on the spline (on the prototype: OPEN 50, CLOSE 120);
find yours with + / - and save them with so / sc. If the servo buzzes at either end it is pushing on
a stop: move that position 1-2 degrees toward the middle. Don't use the Thingy Hook v3 (0-90-180) sketch.

SERVO FASTENING
Option A - bolt: push the M4 nut into its pocket on the inside of the servo wall (turn the frame
  upside down so the top two nuts stay in), put the bolt through the servo ear from outside and
  tighten with a 7 mm wrench or pliers. Snug, then stop - don't crush the ear.
Option B - lock pin: same, but the head has a screwdriver slot. Once tight, push a piece of
  paperclip or thin wire (max 1.5 mm) through the small hole at the tip and bend it over, so the
  nut physically can't unscrew. Like the safety wire on aircraft bolts.
Check before each flight: every nut still tight against the wall and nothing has backed out.
